# mw-image-direction — Catalog

The index for shortlisting: 48 directions in seven groups. One line per direction: what
it feels like, what it is best for, and its variants. Read this first, then open only the
files for the finalists.

## Shortlisting — the axes

Three candidates should differ on **at least two** of these, so picking one teaches the
user something:

| Axis | Ends |
|---|---|
| Medium | photograph · drawn/painted · 3D · flat graphic · mixed/texture |
| Realism | documentary-real · stylized · abstract |
| Energy | calm · loud |
| Temperature | warm · cool |
| Value | light/airy · dark/low-key |
| Era | timeless · a past decade · the future |
| Finish | polished · handmade/imperfect |

*One example, not a default:* for "honest, warm, for a bakery", one good shortlist is
*Lifestyle natural light* (photo, calm, warm), *Children's book* (drawn, handmade),
*Risograph print* (flat graphic, loud-ish, imperfect). Another bakery, with a different
audience, logo or answer to "what should it not look like", gets a different three. A
bad shortlist is three photographic looks with three grades.

## Building the shortlist

- **Match the current look first.** When the brand already has a look (a style guide,
  existing site images, a photo style it keeps), option 1 is *"match the current look"*:
  name its attributes and map them to the nearest direction and variant. The other two
  contrast with it. Brand colors, typefaces and logo rules are kept in every option;
  each brand color takes a role in the palette. A direction that cannot live inside
  the brand palette is flagged once, in its line.
- **Each option carries its recommended shot**, in one line, so one pick settles both:
  *"Analog film · warm consumer color — candid mid-action over the counter, loaf
  lower left"*. Shots: [shots.md](shots.md).
- **Fit the generator.** If the user named the tool they generate with, read each
  finalist's *Engines* line. Skip a direction its notes say that tool handles badly, or
  keep it and say what it costs (a second tool, an editor pass for the type, a cleanup
  step). If the tool is unknown, don't filter; the prompt skill encodes model-neutral.
- **Say what each one is for** in a few plain words, then recommend one.

| techy, futuristic, innovative | Futuristic (clean sci-fi) · Glass morphism · Product UI showcase · Isometric · Aurora · Cyberpunk |
| nostalgic, vintage | Retro (pick the decade) · Analog film · Y2K · Victorian · Art Deco |
| calm, peaceful, soft | Watercolor · Minimalism (Japanese) · Aurora (soft) · Landscape |

| crafty, handmade, organic | Paper craft · Clay (felt and wool) · Print (linocut) · Watercolor · Bohemian |

When they name a format instead of a feeling:

| They ask for | Try |
|---|---|
| a 3D product shot, no photo shoot | Studio product (CGI product render) — for an invented product or a CGI look; a real product is placed from the user's photo, never redrawn |
| headshots, a team page | Editorial fashion (corporate headshot / team portrait) — for real staff, as the shot list for a real shoot |
| a 3D cartoon character, "like an animated film" | Soft 3D (animated-feature 3D character) · Clay |
| a fantasy world, a game environment, a big scene | Painterly (matte painting / environment concept) · Landscape |
| an infographic, explain the numbers | Vector (editorial infographic illustration) · Technical · Isometric |
| a creator-style, "shot on a phone" look | Phone candid (UGC) · Direct flash — never presented as a real customer's photo or review |
| show the app, a SaaS hero | Product UI showcase · Glass morphism · Isometric (tech explainer) |

Dates matter when the user names a decade. Synthwave and vaporwave are 2010s revivals of
80s and 90s imagery, not the decades themselves: for "real 80s", use Memphis, period
photos and print. Frutiger Aero comes after Y2K, about 2004–2013. Bauhaus (1919–33) is
decades older than Swiss Style (1950s on); it shares the geometry, not the era.

The user's own words for a direction ("Editorial", "Retro", "Futuristic") are often
umbrellas. Map them to the specific variant and say so: *"Editorial" could mean
editorial fashion photography, magazine ink illustration or a type-led cover — which?*

## What every direction steers away from

Models fall back on the same safe looks when nothing stops them. A 2025 study in
*Patterns* (Hintze, Proschinger Åström, Schossau) ran an image model and a captioning
model (SDXL and LLaVA) in a loop: each image was captioned, the caption redrawn, for 100
rounds, across 700 runs. Most runs settled into one of twelve motifs: stormy
lighthouses, palatial and gothic interiors, night cityscapes in atmospheric light,
pastoral villages, dramatic landscapes with animals, rustic and industrial vintage
scenes. The authors call it "visual elevator music". The study tested loops, not single
prompts, but it shows where the defaults sit; a prompt that leaves the look open leans
toward them.

The tells to write against, in every direction:

- **Saturation and contrast pushed up** to win a thumbnail.
- **The teal-and-orange grade** on any photograph.
- **Purple-to-blue gradients** and glowing abstract "data" for anything tech.
- **Centered subject, eye level, facing camera, smiling.** The single most common shot.
- **Glossy, poreless skin** and plastic-smooth surfaces.
- **Bokeh, lens flare and volumetric god-rays** added to everything.
- **Symmetric, over-tidy compositions** where nothing overlaps or touches the edge.
- **"Epic" lighting** — rim plus fog plus backlight on a subject that needed none.

Each entry's *slop risks* names the version of this its direction falls into. The fix is
always the same: one more concrete decision, written as a measurement or a named state.
