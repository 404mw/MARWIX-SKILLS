# mw-image-direction — Catalog

The index for shortlisting. One line per direction: what it feels like, what it is best
for, and its variants. Read this first, then open only the files for the candidates.

## Shortlisting — the axes

Three candidates should differ on **at least two** of these, so picking one teaches the
user something:

| Axis | Ends |
|---|---|
| Medium | photograph · drawn/painted · 3D · flat graphic · mixed/texture |
| Realism | documentary-real · stylized · abstract |
| Energy | calm · loud |
| Temperature | warm · cool |
| Value | light/airy · dark/low-key |
| Era | timeless · a past decade · the future |
| Finish | polished · handmade/imperfect |

A good shortlist for "honest, warm, for a bakery": *Lifestyle natural light* (photo,
calm, warm), *Children's book* (drawn, handmade), *Risograph print* (flat graphic,
loud-ish, imperfect). A bad one: three photographic looks with three grades.

The user's own words for a direction ("Editorial", "Retro", "Futuristic") are often
umbrellas. Map them to the specific variant and say so: *"Editorial" could mean
editorial fashion photography, magazine ink illustration or a type-led cover — which?*

## What every direction steers away from

Models fall back on the same safe looks when nothing stops them. A 2025 study in
*Patterns* (Hintze, Proschinger Åström, Schossau) let an image model and a captioning
model describe and redraw each other's output for 100 rounds, across ~1,000 runs; nearly
every run collapsed into one of twelve motifs: stormy lighthouses, palatial and gothic
interiors, night cityscapes in atmospheric light, pastoral villages, dramatic
landscapes with animals, rustic and industrial vintage scenes. The authors call it
"visual elevator music". Unsteered prompts drift the same way.

The tells to write against, in every direction:

- **Saturation and contrast pushed up** to win a thumbnail.
- **The teal-and-orange grade** on any photograph.
- **Purple-to-blue gradients** and glowing abstract "data" for anything tech.
- **Centered subject, eye level, facing camera, smiling.** The single most common shot.
- **Glossy, poreless skin** and plastic-smooth surfaces.
- **Bokeh, lens flare and volumetric god-rays** added to everything.
- **Symmetric, over-tidy compositions** where nothing overlaps or touches the edge.
- **"Epic" lighting** — rim plus fog plus backlight on a subject that needed none.

Each entry's *slop risks* names the version of this its direction falls into. The fix is
always the same: one more concrete decision, written as a measurement or a named state.
