# Directions — Mixed and texture

Four directions built from layering, compositing, surfaces and excess. Shot recipe
names refer to [shots.md](../shots.md); grid terms to
[composition.md](../composition.md).

**For all four:** the risk is noise, not blandness. State a hierarchy — one element
that reads first — and how much of the frame stays quieter.

**Fields.** *Palette* hexes are approximate starting points: a brand's mandated colors
replace the nearest role, and the rest are tuned around them. *Staging depth* names the
row of the depth table in [staging.md](../staging.md) ("When to stage, and how deep").
Collage and maximalism are exempt from the context-item count.

## Collage

**Feels:** layered, editorial, rebellious. **Use for:** covers, campaigns, music, zines,
editorial features. **Not for:** clean product shots or calm UI backgrounds.

- **Palette:** black-and-white photo cutouts plus two or three flat color shapes; or
  faded vintage print color. Approx.: newsprint white `#EDE8DD` (dominant) · photo
  black `#1A1A1A` (secondary, shadow) · halftone gray `#8C8C8C` (secondary) · flat
  orange `#FF5A1F` (accent) · cobalt `#2747D6` (accent).
- **Light:** each cutout keeps its own light; flat paper shadows between layers.
- **Medium & texture:** torn and scissor-cut edges, tape, paper grain, halftone in
  photos, visible layering.
- **Shots:** mixed — a cutout face from a close-up beside a tiny wide-shot figure.
  Scale contrast is the point.
- **Composition:** a clear focal cutout; diagonal arrangement; elements breaking the
  frame edge.
- **Staging depth:** flat — cutouts as frame shares, overlap and paper shadows between
  layers, one focal cutout; each cutout keeps its own light, and wrong scale is the
  point, not an error.
- **Type:** cut-out letters, ransom-note mix, or one bold sans.
- **Variants:**
  - *Cut paper* — scissor-cut colored paper and photo pieces, hand-arranged.
  - *Photo zine* — photocopy black and white, high contrast, tape and staples.
  - *Digital mixed media* — clean cutouts, flat shapes and grain, a polished editorial
    collage.
- **Engines:** every family can assemble it; GPT-Image follows exact arrangements best.
  Never collage real people's photos or copyrighted images the user doesn't own.
- **Slop risks:** random vintage ephemera, butterflies and flowers sprinkled in,
  stamps and postmarks everywhere.

## Graffiti

**Feels:** street, loud, urban. **Use for:** youth, music, sport, events, streetwear.
**Not for:** corporate trust, healthcare, luxury (unless subverting it).

- **Palette:** spray-paint brights with black outlines and white highlights; or a
  stencil's one or two colors on concrete. Approx.: concrete gray `#8E8C88` (dominant)
  · spray lime `#9BE22E` (secondary) · hot pink `#FF2E88` (accent) · white highlight
  `#FFFFFF` (accent) · black outline `#111111` (shadow).
- **Light:** daylight or a streetlight on a real wall.
- **Medium & texture:** overspray, drips, brick and concrete texture, layers of older
  paint and posters underneath.
- **Shots:** flat frontal wall, three-quarter along the wall, wide environmental with
  a person for scale.
- **Composition:** the piece fills the wall; letters overlapping; a character beside.
- **Staging depth:** full when shown as a photo of a real wall (wall and person at real
  sizes, daylight or streetlight on the surface); flat for the artwork alone.
- **Type:** wildstyle or bubble letters — legible if the word matters.
- **Variants:**
  - *Mural piece* — a large painted wall with lettering and characters.
  - *Sticker and tag* — layered stickers, marker tags, poster scraps.
  - *Stencil* — sharp stencilled images in one or two colors.
- **Engines:** GPT-Image or Ideogram if the word must read. Midjourney for the wall
  texture.
- **Slop risks:** unreadable letters, a real artist's tag, generic "street" brick
  wall with a hoodie.

## Maximalism

**Feels:** joyful, abundant, bold. **Use for:** fashion, beauty, events, social,
lifestyle brands with confidence. **Not for:** minimal tech, finance, healthcare.

- **Palette:** many saturated colors with a governing scheme (jewel tones, candy
  brights); patterns that share a color. Approx., jewel tones: emerald `#0F6E54`
  (dominant) · sapphire `#1E3F8A` (secondary) · ruby `#A3133A` (secondary) · saffron
  `#F2A900` (accent) · aubergine `#2B0F2A` (shadow).
- **Light:** bright and even, or a hard flash, so every pattern reads.
- **Medium & texture:** pattern on pattern, rich materials (velvet, lacquer, tile),
  ornaments.
- **Shots:** flat frontal, symmetric center-punch, overhead flat-lay.
- **Composition:** the frame filled edge to edge; a central subject that still reads
  first by scale or contrast.
- **Staging depth:** full when photographed (the *eclectic still life*: sizes, contact,
  even light); flat for pattern and color-block work. Either way, one subject reads
  first.
- **Type:** high-contrast display serif or a chunky decorative face.
- **Variants:**
  - *Pattern clash* — florals, stripes and checks combined on purpose.
  - *Dopamine color* — big blocks of saturated color, playful shapes.
  - *Eclectic still life* — an abundant set: objects, plants, fabrics, ornaments.
- **Engines:** a real beauty or fashion product in the set is placed from the user's
  photo, never redrawn. Midjourney strongest; `--s` higher is fine. For seamless
  patterns, Midjourney with `--tile` (works on V8.1 and V8.2; not with the Edit model).
  GPT-Image for exact object lists.
- **Slop risks:** clutter with no focus, everything the same saturation, pink and gold
  by default.

## Product UI showcase

**Feels:** clear, credible, product-first. **Use for:** SaaS and app heroes, feature
sections, launch posts, app-store and pitch images — the interface is always the user's
own screenshot, composited in the editor, never generated; the model makes only the
device, the set or the background. **Not for:** a product with no interface, or a UI
the user can't supply as screenshots (then draw the idea instead: vector, isometric or
glass morphism, with no claim to be the real screen).

- **Palette:** the UI's own colors carry; the set stays neutral or takes one brand
  hue; the device in a neutral finish. Approx.: warm off-white set `#F2F1EE`
  (dominant) · cool mid gray `#C9CCD1` (secondary) · brand accent, e.g. `#3B6EF5`
  (accent; the brand color goes here) · graphite device `#1C1D21` (shadow).
- **Light:** the screen is a light source: a soft glow on the hands, keyboard and
  surface nearest it, at the UI's color temperature. One soft key for the device body;
  one long highlight along the device edge, kept off the screen itself — a reflection
  across the screen breaks the composite.
- **Medium & texture:** photoreal or clean 3D device, real desk or studio surfaces.
  The screen area is rendered as a flat plate — one even mid-gray or chroma green, no
  reflection, no texture — and replaced in the editor with the real screenshot,
  corner-pinned to the screen's perspective.
- **Shots:** flat frontal; three-quarter product with the screen within ~15° of facing
  the camera (beyond that the UI foreshortens and stops reading); tight detail of a
  hand on the device; over-the-shoulder at a desk with the screen still near-frontal.
- **Composition:** the screen is the hero and the brightest area; one device, or a
  group read as one (laptop with a phone in front, the laptop leading); the UI's key
  element big enough to read at the placement's display size; a quiet zone for the
  headline beside the device, not over the screen.
- **Staging depth:** full — real device sizes (phone ≈ 15 × 7 cm, 13–16″ laptop, 27″
  monitor ≈ 61 × 36 cm), contact shadow under the device, the screen angle, screen glow
  as one of the lights; see [staging.md](../staging.md) "Screens and devices".
- **Type:** the site's own typeface, set live in HTML or the editor; none in the render.
- **Variants:**
  - *Device in context* — a laptop or phone on a real desk or in a hand, the room soft
    behind, window light plus the screen's glow.
  - *Device on a color field* — the device alone on a plain brand-tinted backdrop,
    one soft shadow; floating is licensed if a soft shadow beneath marks the ground.
  - *UI cards* — crops of the real screenshot as separate cards at slightly different
    depths over a soft backdrop, each with its own shadow; no device.
- **Engines:** generate the set with the flat screen plate and composite in the
  editor; an edit model given the screenshot may redraw its text, so check every
  character or keep the screen out of the edit. On every family, state the screen angle
  and "the screen is one flat even gray, no reflection". Midjourney — `--no text,
  interface` keeps it from inventing a UI.
- **Slop risks:** an invented dashboard with gibberish charts, a holographic UI
  floating in the air, glare across the screen, a device turned so far the UI can't be
  read, three devices fanned out, a coffee cup and a plant beside every laptop.
